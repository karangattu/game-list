Small games, real stakes

The arcade has 13 browser games about places around San Francisco Bay. You choose a habitat for a bird or look for a gap in traffic for a newt.

The clue is a drawing of two orange feet. A flap of skin stretches between the toes. You have four habitats to choose from. One is a freshwater pond. Pick it in Sole Clue and the answer shows a mallard in the water. You can see how the webbing turns each foot into a paddle.

Another drawing shows two toes that point forward and two that point back. Those toes grip bark while an acorn woodpecker taps an acorn into a hole for storage. The bird's name comes after the guess. You start with a part of the bird you can look for outside.

How to recognize a bird

In Birdle, a backyard bird appears and you have a few seconds to pick its name. A wrong guess gives you a reason to look more closely the next time that bird appears. Birdle: After Dark gives you a different set of clues. A call or a silhouette can be enough to recognize a bird at night.

In Marsh Madness, you sweep a spotting scope across the marsh to find 12 birds. In Keeping Up with the Kestrels, you identify and count raptors as they fly past. You have to keep a tally even as more birds come into view. What's on the Menu? gives you time to study a beak. Its shape can help you guess what the bird eats.

Where a rail goes at high tide

When a king tide floods the low part of a marsh, there are fewer places for a Ridgway's rail to hide. Tall native plants on higher ground can give this endangered bird cover. The San Francisco Bay Bird Observatory restores vegetation along marsh edges as part of its tidal marsh work.

In Rail Refuge: High Tide Rising, you have a limited supply of seeds. A single patch can shelter a bird for part of the crossing. Several patches close together can give it a route to higher ground. The tide keeps moving. If you plant too low, the water can cut off the route you just made.

In the Bay, the Invasive Spartina Project builds small islands and plants them with marsh gumplant and other native plants. The islands sit higher than the surrounding marsh. As the plants grow, they give rails a place to hide at high tide. These sites are part of the project's work with native plants.

Plant or Pull gives you one marsh plant at a time. You swipe right to plant a native species and left to pull an invasive one. A hint can help you recognize a plant you miss. On a real restoration site, people also return to see how the native plants grow and to look for invasive ones.

A road between newts and water

In the rainy season, newts head down to Lexington Reservoir to breed. Alma Bridge Road crosses their route near Los Gatos. Researchers estimate that nearly 14,000 adult California newts tried to cross from November 2020 to March 2021. Vehicles killed almost 40 percent of them, according to the Midpen study summary.

In Newt Crosser, you start on one side of the road and wait for a gap. Once you get through the first lane, there is traffic in the next one. The tally at the end means more after you try to get a newt past a truck.

Save the Newts models how volunteers can change the survival rate. Save the Newts 3D gives you a flashlight and puts you beside the road at night. On Alma Bridge Road, Newt Patrol volunteers record what happens to the real newts. They share what they find with the team working on a safer crossing.

A shift at Hope's Corner

Hope's Corner serves meals and offers showers and laundry in Mountain View. The work starts before a guest sits down to eat or gets a clean towel. Volunteers prepare food, wash clothes, and stock supplies. The organization describes those jobs on its volunteer page.

In Pantry to Plate, your first crate of donated food can become a batch of tacos. You sort the ingredients before you can cook. As soon as the first dish is ready, a guest arrives. Once the line grows, you have to keep serving people while you prepare the next batch.

In Shower & Supplies, you can start a load of laundry while someone waits for a shower. You clean the stall after each guest uses it. Someone else can arrive just to ask for supplies.

The mallard answer in Sole Clue points you to Lake Elizabeth in Fremont. On your next walk past a pond, watch how a mallard pushes through the water.
